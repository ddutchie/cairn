## What's new in Cairn 3.3.0

### Added

#### People

- **People is part of Cairn.** It no longer sits behind a preview switch: the People view is in the sidebar for everyone (⌘7), the `people` tool, `@` mentions in chat, meeting attendees and owners on tasks are on, and Today's 1:1 prep and nudges work out of the box. Hide the view any time in Settings → Sidebar & views. If you hid People while it was a preview, it appears once and stays however you set it after that.

- **People pictures in the People view.** A switcher at the top of People adds Org chart and Relationships (moved over from Insights, which keeps them too), plus two new ones: **Teams** groups everyone by team, and **Time zones** lays out each person's working day against yours in a 24-hour strip, with who is at work or asleep right now. Click anyone to open them in a side panel.
- **Rhythm, Bridges and Map.** Three more People pictures. **Rhythm** shows who you are due to see, against the cadence you set. **Bridges** shows the people you reach others through, and who would drop off your map without them. **Map** puts everyone on a world map from their time zone, shades the night, and marks who is probably offline; a slider shows who is online later or earlier.

#### Teams and People pictures

- **Teams you can describe.** A team now has a page: what it owns, who leads it, the team it sits under, its channel and links. Open a team from Teams to read or edit it, add and remove members, or start a new team. Teams nest under the team above them, and the Teams view groups them that way. Teams sync to your other devices.
- **Someone can be on more than one team.** Besides their own team, a person can be in any number of guilds, projects and committees (set a team's kind on its page). Each member can have a role on the team, such as "Tech lead" or "Embedded designer". On a person's page, Teams lists every team they're on, with add and remove.
- **Teams remember who used to be on them.** Taking someone off a team keeps when they joined and left, and the team page lists past members. Moving someone to another team ends their old membership instead of erasing it.
- **Renaming a team keeps everyone on it, on every device.** A team keeps its identity through a rename, so a rename on one device can no longer drop people who were edited on another before they synced. The same team made on two devices becomes one team. If two teams still end up with one name, the team page says so and merges them, and Merge into on a team's page folds one team into another.
- **Merge duplicate people.** When Cairn suggests "Sam" and you already have "Sam Whitfield", choose Same as… on the suggestion, or Duplicate in a person's settings. Their meetings, notes, tasks and teams move over, and the other name becomes an alias.
- **One answer to who manages whom.** Marking someone as your manager or direct report (or mentor, buddy, stakeholder) and the reporting line in Relationships and the org chart are now the same fact. Taking the chip off keeps them as a former manager, and an ended relationship no longer still shows as current. The team page notes when a team's lead isn't on it or nobody on it reports to them.
- **The assistant knows your teams.** The `people` tool lists teams with their kind, lead, members and their roles, and past members (`teams`). It can create or update a team, add and remove members, and merge two teams (`team_upsert`), delete a team (`team_delete`), archive someone (`archive`), merge a duplicate person (`merge`), and record that a relationship ended (`link` with `endedAt`). Ask "who is on Sync?", "put Ana and Ben on Sync, led by Alex" or "Priya stopped being my manager in May".
- **The org chart shows teams.** Each card shows the person's role and team, with initials, and a team picker dims everyone outside that team. A Teams switch draws the team tree instead. Zoom with the buttons or ⌘/Ctrl and scroll.
- **Group the People list your way.** Group by relationship, team, location (with the local time), when you last met, or name. Cairn remembers your choice. You are pinned at the top of the list.
- **Bridges uses the Knowledge Graph's canvas,** so it zooms, pans and remembers where people sat, like Relationships. Anyone who is your only route to someone has a ring; select them and the people who would drop off light up.
- **Zoom the map, or turn the globe.** The map zooms and pans, and a Globe switch shows the Earth facing you; drag to turn it. Night shading and who is online work on both.

#### Project links

- **A project knows where its material lives.** Add a project's Confluence space, Slack channels, Jira project, repo or any other link, and Cairn works out which it is and what a connector needs to find it (a space key, a channel id). Ask "get the latest docs from Confluence" or "which Slack channels are for this project?" and the assistant reads the project's links first instead of searching your connectors from scratch. Links sync to your other devices and come out in a project's markdown export.
- **A Links section on the project Overview.** Paste a URL and Cairn fills in the kind, a label and the key; edit the label, key and what it is for, open it in your browser, or remove it. A small dot shows when a matching connector (Atlassian, Slack, GitHub…) is set up. Web addresses already written in the project description are offered as one-click imports.
- **Agents read and write them too.** `get_context` with a project lists its links, and `upsert_project` takes `addLinks` (repeating a URL updates it) and `removeLinkIds`, in chat, in the coding agent and over the MCP server.

#### MCP server

- **The Cairn MCP server tells agents how to work with it.** Claude Code and other MCP clients now get short instructions when they connect: start with `get_context`, claim the card you're working on with `task_progress` and move it to review or done, file what you find with `create_task`, and keep findings and a session summary in notes with `ensure_note`. You no longer need to spell this out in each repository's AGENTS.md or CLAUDE.md.
- **A leaner `people` tool.** Its definition is about a quarter of its old size, so it costs every request less. `link` takes the relationship type directly (`link: "manager_of"`), and `status: "suggested"` now also means "only fill gaps"; older calls still work.

#### Mobile sync

- **Disconnect and remove from folder.** Settings → Mobile app sync has a new Remove… action next to the sync controls. It disconnects and also deletes this workspace's change log and images from the shared folder, then tells the phone it was removed so it stops re-uploading it (before, a phone kept the workspace alive forever). Your workspace on the computer is untouched; choose the same folder again and everything syncs back.
- **Chats name themselves.** After the first reply, Cairn asks the chat's own model for a short title ("Garden bed plan") instead of showing the first line of your message. If the first message was only "hi", it tries again after your next one. A name you give a chat yourself always stays, and the request shows in Usage as Chat title. If the model can't be reached, the first line stays as before.
- **Archiving or merging people asks first.** In chat, the assistant now asks before it archives a person, merges two people or deletes a team, as it already did before deleting a note or task. Restoring someone and other People edits still go ahead without asking.

#### Arcade

- **A hidden arcade.** Click the workspace icon at the top of the sidebar five times quickly and an Arcade item appears under Workspace. It has five pixel games built from your workspace: **Task Force** (shoot down each project's tasks), **Wayfinder** (sail your notes as islands, deliver today's tasks to their harbours, chart the notes nothing links to), **Inbox Zero** (pin tags beside your board to clear tasks before the deadline), **Stacker** and **Breakout**. The games only read your workspace and never change it. A thin workspace plays with a demo one. Use Hide from sidebar to put it away again.
- **Wayfinder: land on islands.** Slow down beside a note island you haven't explored and a Land at prompt appears (click it or press E). Ashore, the captain walks a trail through a low-camera, pseudo-3D stage built from voxel cliffs, spires, mesas and rubble that fade into haze with distance, a stalactite-hung cave mouth at the shrine, trees and drifting fog banks in that island's biome (misty palm jungle, dusty dunes, freezing fog on a snowfield, or smoke over ash fields). Each island has its own sun side, with lit rock faces, long shadows and light shafts through the fog, the first step starts beside the beached rowboat with surf behind you, and the scenery comes toward you as the captain walks on. The captain wears a gold-trimmed tricorn with a parrot on one shoulder, stands just off the trail so you can see what's ahead, and raises a spyglass while waiting. The stage follows the sea: the sun sinks and shadows lengthen with the sea clock, it rains ashore when a storm is near your boat, the sea's fog clears as you land and again as you sail back, and harbour huts and today's lighthouse turn up on some islands. Rocks, trees and the captain sit in the ground with dark footings and grass over their bases, the ground has patches of the island's other terrain and worn dirt along the trail, and the expedition bar now matches the sea HUD. Every island deals its own walk from 4 to 6 steps, always starting on the beach and ending at a note shrine: longer notes walk further, notes with many links become crossroads with extra signposts, very long notes are deep forests, and lost notes are short, moonlit and haunted by a drowned ghost. Between the beach and the shrine you meet two foes per biome (reef crab or vine snake, sand scorpion or dune beetle, frost wisp or ice golem, cinder imp or magma slug), elder foes in their dens, rope bridges, fords, a hermit, shipwrecks, cairn traps and supply caches. The walk follows your project: a fork offers paths toward the notes this one links to and charts them when you get back, a camp under the project's pennant holds one of its task crates to carry to sea, and an overdue task waits as a foe that, once beaten, eases its storm. Relics now include a storm glass (storms slow you less), a kraken tooth (the kraken lets go once), a lucky coin (more gold ashore) and an iron heart (an extra heart), alongside the old speed, fog and cargo relics. Each step offers two choices: click a card or press 1 and 2, the arrows and Enter, and Esc sails back. Clear the shrine to bank the treasure and chart the island, and the relics you found last for the rest of the day. The sea clock stops while you're ashore. Sailing back early costs a little time and the island's treasure, and losing your last heart washes the captain back aboard with one heart.
- **Wayfinder sails smoothly in large windows.** The sea's fog no longer redraws the whole screen every few frames, which made the game stutter in big windows.
- **Wayfinder opens faster.** Building the sea takes about a quarter of the time it did, part of it happens while the arcade menu is showing, and reopening the game with the same workspace reuses the last sea.
- **The Workspace, an idle office for your workspace.** In the Arcade, The Workspace shows your workspace as a pixel office, drawn in stacked voxels with light and shadow. Each project is a desk: its paper pile grows with its open tasks and its book pile with its notes, and a red tab warns of an overdue card. The people your cards are assigned to sit at the desk they're busiest on, get up for coffee and visit each other. A lit monitor means someone is working there. Sun comes through the windows by day. After dark the room goes dark and each working desk is lit by its own monitor. The office follows your workspace live, so when you or the chat agent changes a task or note, the desks and people move to match. Changes show as they happen: a new task drops onto a desk's pile, a finished one flies to the done board, a move or an edit floats a label, and a note the chat agent is writing shows a writing bubble over its desk. Click a desk to look down at that project's board (cards pinned in cork trays) or its notes, and click a card or note to open it in the side panel with the office still in view. Click a person to see what they're working on. The agent has a robot: it walks to the desk of whatever its tool call touches, changes it makes show in agent blue, and clicking it opens the agent chat. The WEEK tab swaps the floor for the week wall with due tasks pinned on their day and a red overdue tray; clicking a pin opens it.

### Fixed

- **A project renamed on your phone moves its notes folder right away.** The phone can now create and rename projects. When a rename arrives through sync, Cairn moves the project's folder of `.md` files to the new name during that sync, instead of leaving it under the old name until the next launch.

#### People

- **Meeting notes you already had are picked up.** Notes whose `attendees` property was on disk before People read it (imported or edited while People was off) now have their attendees captured once at startup, instead of waiting for the file to change.
- **Add person works from every People view,** the time zone view keeps the minutes of someone's working hours (3:30, not 3:00), and Relationships and Org chart no longer show blank when People isn't available.
- **Opening a person from elsewhere works first time.** Clicking a person in Today, or "Open person" on a chat tool card, used to land on the People list without opening them unless you tried again. People now keeps the person you were sent to.
- **The "scan notes for people" tool call has a plain name.** It read "Used People"; it now reads "Scanned notes for people".
- **Tool cards stay open while the assistant is still working.** Opening a tool card during a running turn could collapse it a moment later. Cairn tool cards, connector cards such as Slack and the plain tool chips (bash, read, edit) now all stay open.

- **Adding an ended relationship again makes it current.** Saying "X is my manager again" used to leave the relationship marked as former.
- **The assistant can't make a team by mistake.** A mistyped parent team used to create a new, empty team; it now asks for an existing one.
- **People the assistant adds become real people.** When the assistant named someone as a meeting attendee, Cairn kept them as a suggestion, and a later "add this person" from chat filled them in but left them a suggestion, so they never reached the list. Adding or updating a person as a stated fact now confirms them. A suggested person's page says so, with Confirm and Dismiss, so opening one from chat no longer looks like they were added.

#### Chat and People

- **The context ring shows the real context size.** A long chat that reloaded from disk could read far over 100% (a 137K context showed as 2.7M of 1M), because it added up every model request in the session. It now reads the latest request, and the prompt cache figures match.
- **Slack profiles fill in.** People's title, pronouns and time zone from Slack never appeared with Slack's own connector, because its reply (labelled text) wasn't recognised. It is now, and profiles that failed before refresh the next time you open the person.
- **Slack tells you when it can't read a profile.** The person page says "Slack profile unavailable" with the reason, and the Slack id field in Edit shows whether their profile was read and when. Status emoji from Slack show as emoji, not `:headphones:`. Every standard emoji name is recognised; a custom one (`:partyparrot:`) shows only the status text.

#### People design pass

- **The person page fits the side panel.** The name is smaller beside the pictures, and the Edit fields stack under their labels instead of squeezing the inputs. The two "Relationship" fields are now "Relationship to you" and "Links to others", and the buttons read "Edit details" and "Edit persona" so you can tell them apart.
- **Time zones puts your closest people first.** You are the first row, tinted, then people grouped by how close they are (direct reports, manager, peers and so on, with anyone on a 1:1 cadence or met most recently first). The new menu above the strip also arranges by time zone, by team or by who is available, and **Available now** hides anyone asleep. Your choice is remembered.
- **One layout across the People pictures.** Teams, Time zones, Rhythm, Bridges and Map share the same page, heading and spacing. The Time zones strip shrinks to fit beside the side panel instead of scrolling sideways, and says "6h shared" or "no shared hours" in words. Rhythm explains its bar, line and dot.
- **People search and filter moved into the list.** They sat in the toolbar and pushed the buttons onto a second row. "Find in notes" is now "Find people in notes".

#### Relationships map

- **The relationships map now uses the Knowledge Graph's canvas.** People and their links are drawn by the same engine as the Knowledge Graph, so the map settles off the main thread, remembers where people sat when you come back, and zooms and labels the same way. Link kinds keep their colours and the chips still filter them; former links stay dashed, "meetings together" lines are thicker for more meetings, and you are drawn larger in the accent colour. Hover a line for its description; click a person to open them and light up who they are linked to.
- **Each person shows their connections.** A person's page, and the side panel, has a Connections picture: they sit in the middle and the people they are linked to (manager, reports, mentor, stakeholders, peers) are drawn around them, coloured by kind. Click a neighbour to go to them. "Their links" adds one more step out, and "Meetings together" adds people they meet with. A person with no links shows nothing.
- **A location fills in the time zone, and puts people on the map in their own city.** Setting a person's location to a city (San Francisco, Montreal, Bengaluru, from a list of about 7,000) fills an empty time zone. You can also type a city into the Time zone box and it is stored as its zone (Montreal is America/Toronto; there is no Montreal zone). The Map pins someone at their own city when it lies in their time zone, instead of the zone's principal city. Autofill from a persona note proposes the zone with the location. A time zone you set yourself is never replaced.
- **Location and Time zone suggest as you type.** In a person's Edit, typing "san fr" offers San Francisco, California, US (biggest cities first, "Portland, OR" narrows it); picking one saves it and fills an empty time zone. The Time zone field suggests zone names (`America/Los_Angeles`) and the zones of matching cities. Free text still saves on Enter or when you leave the field.
- **Review fixes.** A single Slack "Status:" line is no longer taken for a whole profile. Connections labels stay visible in the small graph, a person focused from the keyboard is highlighted on the canvas, directed links (reporting, mentor, buddy, stakeholder) get an arrowhead and read "Ann manages Bo" in the hover text, and the Time zones hour labels thin out when the side panel is open.
- **Wayfinder anchors on the Set Sail screen.** The boat now bobs calmly beside Today lighthouse with fishing and swimming crew animations instead of circling before the voyage starts, and desktop/mobile Arcade share a single engine.
- **New Arcade game: Basecamp.** A sixth cartridge in the hidden Cairn Arcade fuses a cozy stacked-voxel camp builder with a repeating 90-second Dawn-to-Night clockwork mystery. Your projects spawn colourful Lodges and wandering Settlers, overdue tasks grow Twilight Brambles to clear for timber, and solving five valley secrets (carrying Hearth embers to all three Watchfire Braziers, opening the Mill Sluice before Night mist reveals a sunken causeway chest, ringing the East Bell at Dusk for the South Radio Tower, upgrading workshops, and awakening the Fifth Cairn) lights the aurora over camp. Move with WASD/arrows or click-drag and press Space/E (or tap ACT) to interact; L opens your Field Log.
- **New Arcade game: Tideglass.** A seventh cartridge and the Arcade's first traversal game: one long one-way descent down a 1080×1320 lighthouse mountain — no loop, no grind. Start at the summit and route through four terraced cliff passes down the fall line to the ebb flats, now rendered in real chunky low-res 3D via the shared software Z-buffer rasterizer (`raster3d.ts`).
- **New Arcade game: Powder Run + paginated Arcade menu.** An eighth cartridge built on the shared 3D engine: carve down a sweeping 3D halfpipe per Cairn project, collect your project's tasks and high-lip notes, dodge or jump overdue-task boulders, and click/tap/press Space in midair to throw random snowboard tricks for combo multipliers. The Arcade menu now paginates cleanly across pages (`<` / `>` or Left/Right).
- **Powder Run is a skill game now.** Speed builds by pumping down the walls and from clean landings. In the air you pick the trick (arrows on a keyboard, a swipe on touch): 360s that stack into 720s, a backflip, a method grab. Each takes time, and the points only count when you land it. Land mid-rotation and you bail, losing the points, your chain, a heart and half your speed. Three hearts per run (a cleared run gives one back), and a wipeout retries that project. Urgent tasks sit high on the walls and are worth more, and notes float above the lips. Every run has a par time and ends with an S to C grade and bonus. Each project rides at a different time of day (midday, dusk, night with lamps and stars, dawn) under a mountain ridge, with split gates, a checkered finish arch, snowfall and speed lines.
- **Tideglass has a reason to explore between its big moments.** 24 pieces of sea glass sit on cliff lips and ridges off the trail, and every four make the oilskin glide longer. Gliding now runs out and refills on the ground. Touch players get a Hop button (tap to hop, hold to glide). A compass strip shows the places you've seen or been told about, and they glow when their moment in the day is near. The HUD names the next event you know of and its time. The island has a real beach, the Wick can no longer walk on the sea, and the causeway to the flats is stepping stones bared at ebb. The ground follows altitude (sand, dune grass, forest, meadow, slate, snow) instead of a checkerboard, the light changes through the day, and the lighthouse no longer blocks the view.
- **Basecamp is about keeping the light.** By day you clear brambles (your open tasks) for timber, build workshops and feed the hearth. By night the Twilight creeps out of the forest as wisps that dim workshops, regrow brambles and drain the fire. Lit watchfires burn them, workshop settlers keep watch, and the Warden's ember swats them. If the hearth goes out the camp is lost, and each night is harder. See five nights through for the camp to endure. Night is properly dark, with pools of light around every fire. The valley's secrets stay as side paths, and Basecamp opens faster.
- **Arcade text shows its dots and dashes.** Game text with a middle dot, a dash or a percent sign showed a question mark instead.
- **3D arcade games draw faster.** The shared 3D renderer merges voxel faces, draws simpler models in the distance and skips anything off screen, roughly halving frame time in Tideglass and Powder Run.
- **Wayfinder islands are real 3D.** Landing on an island now dives from the sea view down to the captain's shoulder, and the island you walk is the one you saw from the boat, its neighbours on the horizon. A level trail winds up from the beach to the summit, with each encounter standing further up it, and every biome has its own trees, rocks and crags (palms, acacias and cacti, snowy firs, dead trees and glowing vents). It also draws with far fewer calls than the old painted stage, which matters most on phones.
- **The 3D arcade games draw about twice as fast again.** The shared 3D renderer now fills each row of a triangle in one pass instead of testing every pixel around it, which nearly halves frame time in Tideglass, Powder Run and Wayfinder's islands with the same picture. Landing on a Wayfinder island also sets up its terrain about a fifth faster.
- **Wayfinder's boat no longer sails under turtles.** Turtles, surfaced whales, leaping dolphins and the kraken's arms now draw behind the boat when they are further away and in front of it when they are nearer, instead of always on top.
- **Wayfinder islands follow your wikilinks.** A note's `[[wikilinks]]` to other notes in play now count as links, not only its stored links, so far fewer islands are lost and more of them fork toward the notes they mention. A one-link fork's other trail is named for the island (the Vine Tunnel, the Salt Track…) instead of always the Wild Path, and lost islands vary their shore and their ghost. On desktop, a note's size now comes from its body when it's loaded.
- **The next encounter on a Wayfinder island stays in view.** The walking camera turns toward the encounter ahead instead of looking straight down the trail, so a foe around a bend of the path is no longer off the edge of the screen.
- **Wayfinder's dolphins look like dolphins.** They're slimmer with tail flukes, show a dorsal fin and wake while swimming just under the surface, and splash as they leap out and dive back in.
- **Powder Run is now about the gems.** Tasks and notes are the score (100–600 each) and tricks multiply them through your chain, instead of tricks paying most of the points. High and urgent tasks float above the walls and anything you grab in the air is held until you land it cleanly, so a bail can lose it. Boulders now only come from overdue tasks (a few more from the third run) and sit on a pulsing red pad. Grades depend on how much you collected and how cleanly, with no par time, and jumps are a little shorter so a backflip is a real bet. The trick popups rise next to the rider and the top bar is simpler.
- **New arcade game: Angler.** A pixel aquarium you stock by fishing your workspace. Each project is its own pond, seen from above in its own biome, with fish swimming under the surface as voxel shadows. Tap or click the water to cast, wait out the nibbles and strike when the bobber plunges (Space or a click/tap; striking a moment early still counts, and striking during a nibble only makes the fish wary). A hooked fish swims every which way: point the rod the way it swims (arrow keys, or a swipe on touch) until it tires (point it elsewhere and the line snaps), then hold or crank the reel to land it. The catch spins on its card as a voxel model. The tank is side-on with depth: fish further back are smaller and hazier, shadows fall on the sand, and weed sways in front of the glass. Every fish is a task or a note: priority picks the species (minnows to pike), notes are angelfish, puffers and koi, and overdue tasks fight harder and are worth more. Keep a catch in the tank or let it go. The tank stays as you left it between visits, and a fish swims home once its task is done or its note is gone, so finishing work empties the tank over time.
- **Arcade games fixed in many small ways.** Powder Run: notes are only collected from the air, the last heart lost on a landing ends the run before anything else is picked up, a trick swipe is measured from the take-off rather than from where your finger first went down, and the trees along the halfpipe no longer jump as you pass. Basecamp: a new camp no longer walks the Warden toward the last camp's tapped spot, the Field Log wraps long clues so the end of each ("AT NIGHT") stays readable, and the design notes match the two-minute day and what each watchfire covers. Tideglass: the clock holds at the end of the day instead of showing 0:00, the HUD names the next event that comes soonest, Mira's lock can only be beaten with a jammed gear, you pick up the driftwood gear you're standing at, Piotr leaves on the ferry, the crate only moves (and can only be witnessed) once the culprit has walked to the dock, a tap on the sky or on a blocked spot no longer sets off a walk, a tap walks to the ground you pressed even if the camera moved meanwhile, and the notebook always keeps room for the secrets' clues. Wayfinder: a camp crate says it's packed until you bank it at the shrine, the trail redraws when you land further along the coast, no sea goes missing at the edge of the view, and the dive's Sail button and choices answer exactly when they appear. Sparse 3D models no longer vanish in the distance. Angler: one tank is shared by all your workspaces, and opening Angler in another workspace, or having more open tasks in a project than the game shows, or a failed read of the open tasks, no longer sends fish home; a damaged saved fish is skipped instead of breaking the tank; a failed save is retried; a fish you let go goes back to the pond as its task is now; pressing on every nibble can't hold off the bite forever, and a strike exactly at the edge of the early window counts; the reel slows when you stop cranking; a released reel key no longer stays held into the next fight; a full tank's KEEP does nothing instead of letting the fish go; and when every pond is empty the game tells you to let a fish go. Powder Run: high and urgent tasks need a jump too, gems lost to a bail no longer count toward the grade, and the trick hint no longer covers points waiting to bank. Breakout: bricks move with the top bar instead of ending up above the ceiling. Basecamp and Tideglass: a drag in progress when the screen resizes is dropped instead of steering the wrong way.
