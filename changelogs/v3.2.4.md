## What's new in Cairn 3.2.4

### Changed

#### Knowledge Graph

- **Large graphs open and drill in without the long wobble.** The Force layout now settles in the background before it draws, so a big graph appears in place instead of animating its whole layout frame by frame. Going back to a graph you've already seen (reopening the view, or leaving a neighbourhood with Esc) shows it exactly as you left it instead of re-settling it, and "Show neighbourhood" starts from where those nodes sat in the full graph and eases into place. On a 476-node, 2,756-edge test graph, opening goes from about 1.8 s of redrawing to about 0.5 s, and returning to the graph from about 1.5 s to under 0.1 s.
- **Smoother pan and zoom on graphs with many dashed links.** While you drag or zoom the Force view, dashed links (semantic, keyword, co-mention) are drawn as faint solid lines, and get their dashes back as soon as you stop. Dashes were the most expensive thing to redraw on every frame.
- **Smoother pan, zoom and drill-in on large graphs.** On graphs over about 400 nodes, the Force view draws at standard resolution while you pan or zoom, and the Radial view does the same while it animates into a branch. Both return to full sharpness as soon as the motion stops. On a 476-node test graph, Force pan and zoom now hold 60 fps where they dropped to 30–50, and Radial drill-in goes from about 40–47 fps to 54–57.

#### Architecture graph

- **Smoother pan and zoom on large code graphs.** The Agent → Architecture dependency graph now draws at standard resolution while you pan, zoom or while it lays out, once it passes about 300 files or 1,500 links, and sharpens again when it stops.

#### Sync

- **More groundwork for People.** Cairn can now read who attended a meeting note and when it happened from optional `attendees` and `date` properties, and keep those properties up to date. This stays switched off until the People feature launches, so nothing changes on screen yet, and existing `attendees` properties in your vault are left alone. A person listed by more than one source (a note's properties and a calendar event, say) now stays at the meeting until every source drops them, and a removal on one device isn't undone by another. Sync protocol bumped to 6, so devices on older versions will show "needs updating".
- A conflicted copy of a note no longer stays linked to the original note's meeting, so a meeting won't list the same note twice.
- Assistants and connected agents get People tools for the upcoming People feature: looking people up, recording who attended a meeting note and when, and setting a task's owner. They stay hidden until People launches, and a person you mark private keeps their note out of every assistant search and read.
- **Indexing groundwork for People.** Cairn can now note which people a note links to, who owns each checklist item, and who an `Attendees:` line lists, and can show people and meetings in the Knowledge Graph. Like the rest of People, this stays switched off until launch, so nothing changes yet.


#### People (preview, off by default)

- **People view, behind the People preview switch.** A list of the people you work with, grouped by relationship (direct reports, manager, peers, stakeholders, mentors, candidates), with a tray for suggested people, search, and a page per person showing their relationships, a timeline of meetings, mentions and owned tasks, and their persona note. Nothing changes for anyone who hasn't turned the preview on: the view starts hidden and the ⌘3–⌘9 shortcuts stay the same.
- **Persona notes stay out of the way.** While the People preview is on, a person's persona note no longer appears in the Notes list or in note pickers; edit it right on their page, or choose Open in Notes. A "Show people's notes" switch in Notes brings them back. The file stays where it was, so Obsidian, links and search are unaffected.
- **Relationships and org chart in Insights, behind the People preview switch.** Insights gets two more canvases: a relationship map of the people you work with (reporting lines, mentors, buddies, stakeholders, peers, with filters per type and an optional "meetings together" layer) and an org chart built from who manages whom, with you highlighted and anyone outside a reporting line listed underneath. Click a person to open their page. They appear only while the People preview is on.

- **1:1 loop, behind the People preview.** Today shows who is on each meeting and links them to the people you track; a Prep button on a person's page (and an automatic run two hours before a calendar meeting with someone you meet on a cadence) builds a short brief from your last meeting, open items and what they've recently worked on, never activity counts or online time; Today lists "No 1:1 with X in 3 weeks" when a cadence slips; and a person's Slack title, local time and pronouns appear on their page, refreshed in the background and never saved into your notes.
- **Find people in your notes (People preview).** With the People preview on, "Find in notes" in the People view scans your workspace for person notes (tagged "person", in a People folder, or in a `Discussions/<Name>` folder) and meeting notes (an `**Attendees:**` line or `attendees` property), shows what it found with how sure it is, and lets you tick what to bring in. Preview shows exactly what would change before you apply; applying links each person to their existing note, attaches the people a meeting note lists, and assigns the checklist items written with their name. Your notes keep their text. Names that fit two people, such as two notes called "Sam Whitfield" or a bare "Chris" with two Chrises, are left alone until you disambiguate them, and running it again changes nothing.
- **Turning on the People preview in a release build.** Settings → About: click the version line seven times quickly and "Preview features unlocked" appears; the People (preview) switch then shows in Settings → Views on this device. It is stored locally, never synced, and release builds show nothing until then.
- **@ a person in chat and coding sessions (People preview).** Type `@` in the chat box or a coding session to pick someone you track, or write `@Ana`. When you send, Cairn gives the assistant what it knows about them: relationships, recent meetings, open items and last 1:1. Private persona notes stay out, and a name two people share asks you which one.
