## What's new

### Added

- **People is on by default.** The People screens, `@` mentions and the `people` tool no longer need a hidden switch; the Developer switch under Settings → Appearance is gone. The "People preview" label is retired.
- **Add a person from the phone.** The People list has an Add person button beside the grouping menu. Give them a name, a relationship to you (the same choices as the computer), and optionally their role, team, location and time zone. Location and time zone suggest places as you type, and a location fills an empty time zone. "This is me" marks them as you. Cairn opens their page afterwards; email, aliases, stage and cadence are added there.
- **People forms open as full native modals.** Add person, New team, a person's Details and Relationship, adding someone to a team, moving them to a new one, a team's Details and Members, and adding attendees to a note now open the same way Settings and the project form do, instead of a sheet that slides up inside the app. Pickers (who leads, which team it sits under, who this duplicate is) open as a page inside the same modal with a Back button. Location and Time zone suggest places as you type when you edit a person too.
- **Delete a workspace from the phone.** Open the sync screen and swipe a workspace left to delete it. Cairn removes its sync files from iCloud for every device and deletes this phone's copy. If your computer is still connected to the same folder it uploads the workspace again, so deleting also works as a fresh re-sync.
- **Removed on the computer? The phone stops re-uploading it.** When the desktop removes a workspace from the sync folder, the phone pauses syncing it and says so on the sync screen, instead of publishing it back and keeping it alive.
- **Teams sync from the desktop.** A team's details (what it owns, its kind, its lead, the team above it, its channel and links) and who is on it, with their roles and past members, come across with your people. A person's team on their page is their own team; someone can also be in guilds and projects. The assistant's `people` tool can list, update, merge and delete teams here too, merge duplicate people, archive someone and record that a relationship ended.

- **A Workspace tab holds People, the graph, usage and settings.** The tab bar is Projects, Calendar, Chat, Workspace and Search, so nothing hides in More. Each row says what is in it, like "4 people, 2 teams". Press and hold a tool to show it above the tab bar from any other tab, and press and hold that to change or hide it. Opening a person from chat, Today or a note still lands on their page. Its icon is a stack of layers, like the stones of a cairn.
- **A map and a globe of where everyone is.** The People switcher gains a Map segment: a world map you can pinch and drag, or a globe you turn with a finger, with the night side shaded and the sun marked. Slide the time forward or back to see who is online at 3pm tomorrow. Tap a marker to see who is there and open their page.
- **Every tab has the same header, and settings live in one place.** Projects, Calendar, Chat, Workspace and Search all use the small title at the top, so nothing wastes space above the content (the big Workspace title is gone). Settings are now one row in the Workspace tab, grouped like the computer's Settings: App, AI, Extensions and Data. Appearance and Sync no longer sit in the Projects menu and toolbar; Settings is one entry there instead. Usage, Sync, AI settings, Tools and Appearance open inside the Workspace tab with its tab bar. Switch workspace from the top of the Workspace tab.
- **Rhythm and Time zones in People.** Rhythm lists who you are due to meet by the cadence you set (overdue, due soon, on track), and Time zones shows who is working, at the edges of their day or asleep, with their local time and the working hours you share. The People switcher also stays put when you change segment. On the unfolded Duo the pinned tool no longer floats over the bottom of both columns, which left an empty band under them; it is now a button in the toolbar (the side rail) on every tab instead.
- **Time zones puts your closest people first.** You are the first row, tinted, then people grouped by relationship (direct reports, manager, peers and so on, with anyone on a 1:1 cadence or met most recently first). The toolbar menu arranges by closest, time zone, availability or team, and Available now hides anyone asleep. The phone remembers your choice.
- **A 24-hour strip for each person in Time zones.** Under every row, a bar in your own local hours shows when they work, bright where it overlaps your 9 to 5 and grey where it doesn't, with a line for now, like the computer's Time zones view.
- **Group your people five ways.** The grouping button on People switches between relationship, team, location (with each place's local time), last met and name, and remembers your choice on this phone. You stay pinned at the top.
- **Teams.** A People | Teams switcher lists your teams nested under the team they sit in, with who leads each and how many are on it. A team's page shows what it owns, its lead, the teams above and below it, its channel and links, its members with their roles and the people who used to be on it. Edit the details (name, kind, what it owns, lead, the team above, channel, links), add and remove members and set their roles, merge two teams that are one, or delete one, all from the phone.
- **Teams on a person's page.** See the teams someone is on and where they were before, take them off one, add them to another or move them to a new team. Duplicates can be merged from the phone: use Same as… on a suggested person, or Same person as… in a person's details. Former relationships show quietly as former.
- **The model picker moves into the chat box, and Chat loses its header bar.** On iPhone, Chat no longer has a title bar across the top: the conversation runs to the top of the screen, with only the usage ring and Clear chat floating there (Clear chat sits in the side rail on the Duo). While you type, the chat box opens into two rows: the message on top, then `+`, the model and send. With the keyboard down it is one row again. Tap the model to open a panel from it: your favorite models one tap away, Provider and Model (each lists its choices right in the panel), Temperature (a slider from 0 to 1 in steps of 0.1, or Auto), Max steps and AI settings. `+` now opens your four most recent photos (tap one to attach it), Camera, Photos and Add files, then Personality (the panel lists them and you pick one there; when there are no personalities to list, it opens AI settings) and Tools & services. On a model that can't read images the attach rows are hidden, but `+` still opens the rest. Without a model to pick (Apple's on-device model, or Android, where the model menu stays in the header), `+` keeps AI settings.
- **See what the assistant changed.** Tap a Cairn tool in chat to open it: a task's fields as before and after (column, priority, due date), a note edit as removed and added lines, a renamed note, search hits, People and idea-flow changes. A "Raw call" section keeps the arguments and result. Opening the note or task it touched is now an Open button on the card. The phone reports the same changes the computer does, from the same shared code.
- **Settings open the same way from everywhere.** Settings is one sheet with Appearance, AI settings, Tools and services and Sync inside it, whether you open it from Workspace, the Projects menu, Chat or the sync badge, so the Projects menu no longer switches you to the Workspace tab. Usage now stays inside the Workspace tab with its tab bar. AI settings is now a short list instead of two tabs: the everyday tuning (temperature, max steps, personality, renderer, chat theme) is right there, and Provider & model, with its keys, is one tap away.
- **Notes, tasks and projects open inside the tab you came from.** Opening one from Search, Calendar, the knowledge graph, People or a Projects list now keeps the tab bar, and Back returns you to the screen you were on. Before, only Projects did this; the others covered the tab bar with a full-screen page.
- **Tags, Data and Writing style in Settings.** Tags lists every tag with how many notes and tasks use it; tap one to rename it, pick a new colour or delete it, and the change syncs to your other devices. Data shows how much space this phone uses and how many projects, notes, tasks, tags and people it holds. Writing style shows the persona and guides you set on your computer, so you can see what chat drafts from (editing stays on the computer for now).
- **Today, in the Workspace tab.** The first tool in the list: tasks overdue and due today, today's meetings, and people you are overdue to meet, all from what syncs to the phone. Press and hold it to show it above the tab bar, where it reads like "2 overdue, 3 due today, 1 meeting". The assistant can answer "what's on today" too (`get_today`). It is the phone's own view; the computer's AI brief does not sync yet.
- **Create and edit projects on the phone.** New project is in the Projects menu (and on the empty Projects screen): give it a name, an icon, a status, a priority and a description, and it starts with the usual board columns (Backlog, Todo, In Progress, Review, Done), the same as on the computer. Edit project on a project's toolbar changes the same fields. The assistant can do both too (`upsert_project`). Everything syncs to your other devices.
- **The assistant can ask before it acts.** When it needs a few details, it shows a short form in the chat: one answer box per question, sent together with Send answers, and it carries on with your answers in the same reply (`ask_questions`). Skip closes the form, and the assistant goes on with its best guess.
- **Deletes and other removals wait for your tap.** When the assistant wants to delete a note or task, archive a person, merge two people or delete a team, a card in the chat asks first. Nothing changes until you tap the button, and Cancel leaves it alone (the trail then reads "Kept note …" or "Didn't archive …"). The same rule decides this on desktop.
- **Your day on Today, with the free time left.** Today's meetings now sit in a day strip from 8 to 6: busy blocks, the gaps between them, the longest gap still ahead marked, and a line like "2h 30m free, longest 2h". Gaps already gone are hidden.
- **You owe, on Today.** Open action items assigned to you across all your notes, with a tick to mark one done and a tap to open the note. They count in the headline and in the assistant's `get_today`, which now also returns your free time. It needs you marked as "This is me" in People.
- **Add your own MCP server.** Under Settings → Tools, **Add your own** takes a server URL (https) for anything that isn't in the registry. For a provider with no automatic registration, such as a Microsoft 365 tenant, paste the client ID of your own app registration and, if you need one, its redirect address. Sign-in uses PKCE, so no secret is stored. It stays on this phone and is never synced. API-key servers aren't covered yet.
- **Connector calls ask first.** Before the assistant runs a tool on an MCP server or HTTP service, a card names the connector and shows what will be sent. Allow runs it once; Always allow stops asking about that tool until you clear the chat or restart the app. The computer already worked this way.
- **Project links on the Overview.** A project's Confluence space, Slack channels, Jira project, repo and other links, set on the desktop, show on its Overview. Tap one to open it.
- **A new hidden game: Task Force.** Tap the Cairn icon on an empty screen five times to open the Cairn Arcade and pick Task Force: a pixel-art vertical shooter over procedurally generated islands. Each project with open tasks is a level. Its tasks fly in as enemy ships, bigger and tougher the higher their priority, and every one you shoot down shows as done. Notes float by as pickups that give you a spread shot, tasks that slip past come back later in the wave, and a project review boss ends each level. Drag anywhere to steer; it fires on its own. Your best score stays on this phone. Nothing in the game changes your tasks.
- **The Cairn Arcade, and pixel-art Wayfinder, Inbox Zero, Stacker and Breakout.** The secret five-tap menu is now a pixel-art arcade with five games built from your workspace: **Task Force**, **Wayfinder** (sail your notes as islands, deliver today's tasks to their harbours, chart unlinked notes in the fog), **Inbox Zero** (pin tag towers beside your board flow to triage tasks before the deadline), **Stacker** and **Breakout**. All the games keep their text clear of the screen's rounded corners, including on the unfolded iPhone Duo, and Close sits in the side rail there.

- **New hidden game: Basecamp.** The Cairn Arcade adds a sixth cartridge fusing a cozy voxel camp builder with a repeating Dawn-to-Night clockwork mystery: walk the lantern Warden around camp with one thumb (drag to steer or tap to walk, tap ACT beside objects), clear overdue-task Twilight Brambles for timber, upgrade your project Lodges, talk to Settlers for schedule hints, and unlock five valley secrets before awakening the Fifth Cairn.
- **New hidden game: Tideglass.** The Arcade adds a seventh cartridge and its first traversal game: one long one-way descent down a huge lighthouse mountain — no loop, no grind. Start at the summit and route through four terraced cliff passes down the fall line to the ebb flats, rendered as a fixed-yaw oblique voxel diorama with riser faces, occluding cliff walls, foam-ringed water, mist banks and a beacon cone. Four islanders keep regional routines with cliff-aware pathfinding, and tap-to-walk routes around the walls for you. The island variation — gear wash-ups, culprit, flare frequency, crew colours — is seeded by your workspace. Jam the sluice so the bridge holds, intercept the flare on the right frequency, trace the moved cargo crate, hold the lamp, and open the ebb causeway to light the Tideglass and complete the descent.

### Fixed

- **No more "index" flash at launch.** The entry screen that sends you to Projects no longer shows a header for a moment as the app opens.
- **Opening a project no longer crashes the app.** The big percentage on a project's overview and the Projects header card lined up their digits with the % sign by text baseline, which React Native can abort on while the counter is animating. They now align at the bottom edge.

- **Renaming a team on one device no longer drops people edited on another.** Teams keep their identity through a rename.
- **People the assistant adds become real people.** Adding or updating a person as a stated fact now confirms someone who was only suggested (for example, a meeting attendee name), so they appear in your list.
- **Wayfinder anchors on the Set Sail screen.** The boat bobs in place with fishing and swimming crew instead of spinning in a circle, and desktop and mobile Arcade now run the same shared game engine and renderers.
- **Wayfinder: land on islands.** Slow down beside a note island you haven't explored and a Land at prompt appears (tap it). Ashore, the captain walks a trail through a low-camera, pseudo-3D stage built from voxel cliffs, spires, mesas and rubble that fade into haze with distance, a stalactite-hung cave mouth at the shrine, trees and drifting fog banks in that island's biome (misty palm jungle, dusty dunes, freezing fog on a snowfield, or smoke over ash fields). Each island has its own sun side, with lit rock faces, long shadows and light shafts through the fog, the first step starts beside the beached rowboat with surf behind you, and the scenery comes toward you as the captain walks on. The captain wears a gold-trimmed tricorn with a parrot on one shoulder, stands just off the trail so you can see what's ahead, and raises a spyglass while waiting. The stage follows the sea: the sun sinks and shadows lengthen with the sea clock, it rains ashore when a storm is near your boat, the sea's fog clears as you land and again as you sail back, and harbour huts and today's lighthouse turn up on some islands. Rocks, trees and the captain sit in the ground with dark footings and grass over their bases, the ground has patches of the island's other terrain and worn dirt along the trail, and the expedition bar now matches the sea HUD. Every island deals its own walk from 4 to 6 steps, always starting on the beach and ending at a note shrine: longer notes walk further, notes with many links become crossroads with extra signposts, very long notes are deep forests, and lost notes are short, moonlit and haunted by a drowned ghost. Between the beach and the shrine you meet two foes per biome (reef crab or vine snake, sand scorpion or dune beetle, frost wisp or ice golem, cinder imp or magma slug), elder foes in their dens, rope bridges, fords, a hermit, shipwrecks, cairn traps and supply caches. The walk follows your project: a fork offers paths toward the notes this one links to and charts them when you get back, a camp under the project's pennant holds one of its task crates to carry to sea, and an overdue task waits as a foe that, once beaten, eases its storm. Relics now include a storm glass (storms slow you less), a kraken tooth (the kraken lets go once), a lucky coin (more gold ashore) and an iron heart (an extra heart), alongside the old speed, fog and cargo relics. Each step offers two choices on a parchment card, and the phone taps for each step, strike and find. Clear the shrine to bank the treasure and chart the island, and the relics you found last for the rest of the day. The sea clock stops while you're ashore. Sailing back early costs a little time and the island's treasure, and losing your last heart washes the captain back aboard with one heart.
- **Wayfinder sails smoothly on big screens.** The sea's fog no longer redraws the whole screen every few frames, which made the game stutter on the unfolded iPhone Duo.
- **Wayfinder opens faster.** Building the sea takes about a quarter of the time it did, part of it happens while the arcade menu is showing, and reopening the game, or folding and unfolding the iPhone Duo mid-game, reuses the last sea instead of building it again.
- **Search keeps its tab bar and buttons after a game on the iPhone Duo.** Opening the arcade from Search, unfolding or folding the phone, and closing the game used to leave Search without its tab bar, with the semantic toggle missing and an empty button in the header. The search screen now catches up with the new screen size once the game has closed.
- **New Arcade game: Powder Run + paginated Arcade menu.** Carve down a 3D snow halfpipe per project, collect your project's tasks and high-lip notes, and tap in midair to throw random snowboard tricks for combo points. The Arcade cartridge menu now paginates cleanly with `<` / `>` controls.
- **Powder Run is a skill game now.** Speed builds by pumping down the walls and from clean landings. In the air you pick the trick (arrows on a keyboard, a swipe on touch): 360s that stack into 720s, a backflip, a method grab. Each takes time, and the points only count when you land it. Land mid-rotation and you bail, losing the points, your chain, a heart and half your speed. Three hearts per run (a cleared run gives one back), and a wipeout retries that project. Urgent tasks sit high on the walls and are worth more, and notes float above the lips. Every run has a par time and ends with an S to C grade and bonus. Each project rides at a different time of day (midday, dusk, night with lamps and stars, dawn) under a mountain ridge, with split gates, a checkered finish arch, snowfall and speed lines.
- **Tideglass has a reason to explore between its big moments.** 24 pieces of sea glass sit on cliff lips and ridges off the trail, and every four make the oilskin glide longer. Gliding now runs out and refills on the ground. Touch players get a Hop button (tap to hop, hold to glide). A compass strip shows the places you've seen or been told about, and they glow when their moment in the day is near. The HUD names the next event you know of and its time. The island has a real beach, the Wick can no longer walk on the sea, and the causeway to the flats is stepping stones bared at ebb. The ground follows altitude (sand, dune grass, forest, meadow, slate, snow) instead of a checkerboard, the light changes through the day, and the lighthouse no longer blocks the view.
- **Basecamp is about keeping the light.** By day you clear brambles (your open tasks) for timber, build workshops and feed the hearth. By night the Twilight creeps out of the forest as wisps that dim workshops, regrow brambles and drain the fire. Lit watchfires burn them, workshop settlers keep watch, and the Warden's ember swats them. If the hearth goes out the camp is lost, and each night is harder. See five nights through for the camp to endure. Night is properly dark, with pools of light around every fire. The valley's secrets stay as side paths, and Basecamp opens faster.
- **Arcade text shows its dots and dashes.** Game text with a middle dot, a dash or a percent sign showed a question mark instead.
- **3D arcade games draw faster.** The shared 3D renderer merges voxel faces, draws simpler models in the distance and skips anything off screen, roughly halving frame time in Tideglass and Powder Run.
- **Wayfinder islands are real 3D.** Landing on an island now dives from the sea view down to the captain's shoulder, and the island you walk is the one you saw from the boat, its neighbours on the horizon. A level trail winds up from the beach to the summit, with each encounter standing further up it, and every biome has its own trees, rocks and crags (palms, acacias and cacti, snowy firs, dead trees and glowing vents). It also draws with far fewer calls than the old painted stage, which matters most on phones.
- **The 3D arcade games draw about twice as fast again.** The shared 3D renderer now fills each row of a triangle in one pass instead of testing every pixel around it, which nearly halves frame time in Tideglass, Powder Run and Wayfinder's islands with the same picture. Landing on a Wayfinder island also sets up its terrain about a fifth faster.
- **Wayfinder's boat no longer sails under turtles.** Turtles, surfaced whales, leaping dolphins and the kraken's arms now draw behind the boat when they are further away and in front of it when they are nearer, instead of always on top.
- **Wayfinder islands follow your wikilinks.** A note's `[[wikilinks]]` to other notes in play now count as links, not only its stored links, so far fewer islands are lost and more of them fork toward the notes they mention. A one-link fork's other trail is named for the island (the Vine Tunnel, the Salt Track…) instead of always the Wild Path, and lost islands vary their shore and their ghost. On desktop, a note's size now comes from its body when it's loaded.
- **The next encounter on a Wayfinder island stays in view.** The walking camera turns toward the encounter ahead instead of looking straight down the trail, so a foe around a bend of the path is no longer off the edge of the screen.
- **Wayfinder's dolphins look like dolphins.** They're slimmer with tail flukes, show a dorsal fin and wake while swimming just under the surface, and splash as they leap out and dive back in.
- **Unfolding the Duo no longer restarts the game.** Folding or unfolding now resizes a running game in place, so a Wayfinder voyage, a Powder Run, a Tideglass descent and the rest carry on where you were instead of starting over, with the HUD moving to the new screen edges.
- **Powder Run is now about the gems.** Tasks and notes are the score (100–600 each) and tricks multiply them through your chain, instead of tricks paying most of the points. High and urgent tasks float above the walls and anything you grab in the air is held until you land it cleanly, so a bail can lose it. Boulders now only come from overdue tasks (a few more from the third run) and sit on a pulsing red pad. Grades depend on how much you collected and how cleanly, with no par time, and jumps are a little shorter so a backflip is a real bet. The trick popups rise next to the rider and the top bar is simpler.
- **New arcade game: Angler.** A pixel aquarium you stock by fishing your workspace. Each project is its own pond, seen from above in its own biome, with fish swimming under the surface as voxel shadows. Tap or click the water to cast, wait out the nibbles and strike when the bobber plunges (Space or a click/tap; striking a moment early still counts, and striking during a nibble only makes the fish wary). A hooked fish swims every which way: swipe the rod the way it swims until it tires (point it elsewhere and the line snaps), then hold or crank the reel to land it. The catch spins on its card as a voxel model. The tank is side-on with depth: fish further back are smaller and hazier, shadows fall on the sand, and weed sways in front of the glass. Every fish is a task or a note: priority picks the species (minnows to pike), notes are angelfish, puffers and koi, and overdue tasks fight harder and are worth more. Keep a catch in the tank or let it go. The tank stays as you left it between visits, and a fish swims home once its task is done or its note is gone, so finishing work empties the tank over time.
- **Arcade games are Workspace tools once you've found the arcade.** After the five-tap unlock, the Workspace tab lists the Arcade and each game under Tools, with your best score. Press and hold one to pin it above the tab bar like any other tool.
